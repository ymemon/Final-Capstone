What is a PET Scan?

A positron emission tomography (PET) scan can be used alongside other cancer treatments to achieve the best results. For some types of cancer, it helps identify where the cancer is located and its stage. Knowing this allows your doctor to determine the most effective treatment plan for you.

PET Scan – Frequently Asked Questions

A PET scan helps your doctor detect cancer, determine its stage, and monitor how well your treatment is working. It provides detailed images that reveal how your organs and tissues are functioning.

No, a PET scan is not painful. The procedure involves a small injection of a radioactive tracer, followed by a rest period before the scan. Most patients find it simple and comfortable.

The full process typically takes about 1 to 2 hours, including preparation time. The actual scanning portion usually lasts between 20 to 40 minutes.

PET scans are very safe. The amount of radioactive tracer used is extremely small and leaves the body naturally within a short period of time. Your physician will ensure it’s appropriate for you.
