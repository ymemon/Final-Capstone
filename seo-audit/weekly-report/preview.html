In plain English

Over these four weeks your site was shown in Google search results 31,078 times and people clicked through to it 189 times. Being shown is called an impression; it means your page appeared on a results page somebody looked at, whether or not they clicked.

Your average position was 25.6, which is several pages deep, where almost nobody scrolls. Most clicks in Google go to the top three or four results. That number is the average ranking across every search you appeared in, so it moves slowly and a change of a point or two is normal noise rather than a signal.

Of everyone who saw you, 0.61% clicked. That is the click-through rate, and it is mostly a measure of whether your title and description in the results made the page look worth opening. A low rate alongside high impressions usually means you are being found for searches that are not quite what you do.
