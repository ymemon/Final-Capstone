What you are actually buying

Search rankings are not for sale. Google does not accept payment for organic placement, and no agency has a back channel. What you are buying is labor across four areas, in roughly this order of priority:

1. Removing the things blocking you

Sites frequently carry a specific, fixable defect that suppresses everything else: a stray noindex tag, a redirect chain, a slow server, a plugin blocking crawlers, a sitemap listing pages that no longer exist. This work is diagnostic rather than creative, and when a blocker is present, fixing it outperforms everything else on this list combined.

2. Local search presence

For businesses serving a geographic area, the Google Business Profile is usually worth more than the website. Primary category, service areas, hours, photos, review volume and recency, and consistent business details across every directory that lists you. This is where most Arizona small businesses have the largest untapped gap.

3. Pages that match real searches

One page cannot rank for six different services. Each thing people search for separately needs a page written for that search, in the words people actually use rather than internal terminology. This is the bulk of ongoing work on most engagements.

4. Authority over time

Other sites linking to yours remains a significant ranking factor. Legitimately this means being genuinely worth citing — real work, real results, real local involvement. There is a large industry selling purchased links; it works until it does not, and the recovery from a manual penalty costs more than the shortcut saved.

How SEO fits with everything else

SEO is one input. It interacts with the rest of your web presence, and treating it in isolation wastes money:

  • Your site itself. Rankings that send visitors to a site that does not convert produce traffic and no revenue. If the underlying site is the constraint, that is the thing to fix first.
  • Hosting and speed. Page speed is a measured ranking factor and an even larger conversion factor. A site on overloaded shared hosting is fighting its own infrastructure — see hosting plans.
  • Security. A compromised site can be removed from results entirely and shown as a warning screen to anyone who tries to visit. Recovery takes far longer than prevention — see website security.
  • Paid search. Complementary, not competing. Paid delivers now, SEO compounds later. The usual pattern is paid while organic matures, tapering as organic takes over.

FAQ

What is the difference between local SEO and regular SEO?

Local SEO targets searches with geographic intent and competes for the map pack, where proximity and your Google Business Profile carry most of the weight. Organic SEO competes for the standard results below it, where content and links matter more. Most Arizona service businesses need both, weighted toward local.

How much of SEO is technical versus content?

It depends entirely on the site's condition. A site with a crawl-blocking misconfiguration needs technical work before content is worth writing. A technically clean site with three thin pages needs content. We find out which you are before proposing either.

Will you work with my existing web developer?

Yes, and often that is the cheaper arrangement. If you already have someone maintaining the site, we can hand them a prioritized list of changes rather than duplicating a role you are already paying for.

What happens in the first conversation?

We look at your site, your current rankings and your competitors, then tell you what we would do first and roughly what it would cost. It takes about half an hour and there is no charge, because we would rather find out early whether the engagement makes sense for either of us.
