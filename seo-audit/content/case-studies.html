Technical SEO Everything IT Location-page consolidation with measured Search Console results Technology: WordPress, Google Search Console, 301 redirects Outcome: Clicks +32%, impressions +18%

Everything IT — consolidating a sprawling location-page structure

Client: Everything IT, a managed IT services provider based in Dublin, serving business clients across Ireland.
Work: Technical SEO, information architecture, content.
Period measured: 1–27 July 2026 against 1–27 August 2026, from Google Search Console.

The problem

The site had accumulated location pages the way most sites accumulate them: gradually, by different people, with no plan. Multiple URLs targeted the same city — /cork/, /it-support-cork/ and /it-support-cork-2/ all existed simultaneously. The copy across them was close to identical with the city name swapped, which is the doorway pattern Google filters rather than rewards. The pages competed with each other for the same searches, and none of them won.

What we did

  • Cut the location pages down to four. Cork, Galway, Limerick and Waterford — the four regions where Everything IT genuinely has something specific to offer. Everything else was retired and redirected rather than left to dilute the site.
  • Wrote each of the four differently. Not a template with the city swapped. Cork City and County Cork, Galway and the West, Limerick and the Mid-West, Waterford and the South-East each serve a different mix of local businesses, so each page targets the services that actually match its area.
  • Resolved the duplicate URLs. One canonical page per location, with 301s from the retired variants so the accumulated equity moved rather than evaporated.
  • Rebuilt internal linking between the location pages and the location directory, so the four surviving pages inherited the internal authority the retired ones had been splitting.

What did not go to plan

Two things, stated plainly because a case study that reports only the good half is an advertisement.

Limerick's average position got worse — 17.5 to 27.8 — while its impressions more than doubled. Those two facts are related. The page went from surfacing for a narrow set of searches to surfacing for a much wider set, and the newly-added ones sit lower down. An average dragged down by winning more ground is not the same as a decline, but it looks identical on a dashboard, and any agency reporting on this account should be able to tell you which one you are looking at.

Cork and Waterford both lost impressions even as their positions improved sharply. During a consolidation the retired URLs keep appearing in search results for a while, then stop; the totals dip before they recover. We expected this and said so in advance, which is the only reason it was not alarming when it happened.

Across the whole domain, including every retired URL bleeding its old traffic through a redirect, the same month looked flat. Judged only on the four pages that were actually the subject of the work, it did not. Both numbers are true, and an agency that shows you only the flattering one is choosing which truth to hand you.

Web design & development Prestige Windows A ground-up WordPress website for an Arizona window company Technology: WordPress, Elementor, PHP, CSS Status: Website live; SEO started September 9, 2026

Prestige Windows — a ground-up website for an Arizona window company

Client: Prestige Windows, an Arizona window and door company.
Work: Website strategy, visual design, user experience, WordPress development, content structure and lead capture.
Build launched: May 2026.
SEO phase: Began September 9, 2026. The baseline and measured results will be added here as the data matures.
Website: prestigewindowsaz.com

The starting point

This was a ground-up website project, not a redesign of an existing Prestige Windows site. The job was to turn a premium window and door offering into a clear digital experience: establish the visual direction, organize a broad product range, help homeowners understand their options and give them straightforward ways to request a quote or consultation.

What AZWebCorp built

  • A distinct visual system. Deep charcoal, warm gold, architectural typography and large-format imagery give the site a high-end identity suited to the products Prestige sells.
  • A complete WordPress site structure. The build includes Home, About, Products, Window Configurator, Gallery, Blog, FAQ and Contact experiences rather than a single brochure page.
  • Product education. Visitors can explore window systems, glass options and signature collections before they are asked to contact the business.
  • Multiple lead paths. Consultation booking, quote requests, phone contact and the window configurator give people more than one practical way to take the next step.
  • A responsive, maintainable foundation. The site works across desktop and mobile, and its WordPress structure gives the business room to add project work, service content and search-focused pages without rebuilding the platform.
  • Technical search foundations. Page-level metadata controls, structured data, service-area signals and deliberate index controls provide the starting point for the SEO phase that follows the build.

The result at launch

The result at this stage is the working website itself. It is live, publicly inspectable and gives prospective customers clear routes from initial product research to a consultation or quote request. That is a website-build outcome; it is not being presented as proof of an SEO increase.

SEO phase — started September 9, 2026

The search campaign begins with a dated baseline before conclusions are drawn. AZWebCorp SEO Client Portal — one private dashboard per client

Product: AZWebCorp's in-house SEO reporting platform.
Work: Product strategy, interface design, full-stack development, Google data integrations and client-specific access.
Technology: React 19, TypeScript, Vite, Cloudflare Workers, Cloudflare D1, Drizzle ORM, Recharts, Google Search Console and Google Analytics 4.

The problem

SEO reporting is usually scattered across separate products, emailed spreadsheets and occasional screenshots. A client may know that work is happening without having a simple place to see what changed, where traffic came from, which pages are moving and what deserves attention next.

What AZWebCorp built

The portal gives each client a private workspace that brings the reporting layers together under one roof. The dashboard opens with the current signal rather than a wall of raw exports, then lets the client move into the detail when they need it.

  • One performance dashboard for Search Console clicks, impressions, click-through rate, average position and ranking-keyword coverage alongside GA4 users and sessions.
  • A live activity view showing current visitors, active pages, locations and devices when Google Analytics data is available.
  • Dedicated work areas for keywords, landing pages, opportunities, technical checks, Google Business, AI visibility, competitor analysis and reports.
  • Practical date controls and exports so a client can compare recent periods and take a copy of the data without asking the agency to assemble it manually.
  • Separate client access so every business opens its own reporting workspace rather than seeing a shared agency dashboard or another client's information.

The result

Clients no longer have to translate a collection of disconnected reports into a picture of performance. They can sign in, see the current executive signal, inspect the supporting Search Console and Analytics data, and move from the overview into the specific area they care about. It makes ongoing SEO work easier to understand without pretending that every useful detail belongs on the first screen.

What AZWebCorp built

The result is a working public product: anyone can visit azwebcorp.com/seo, test a public website and move from a high-level score into the evidence behind it. The audit makes technical findings easier to discuss because the visitor and the agency can begin with the same dated report.
