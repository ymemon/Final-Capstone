Everything IT — consolidating a sprawling location-page structure

Client: Everything IT, a managed IT services provider based in Dublin, serving business clients across Ireland.
Work: Technical SEO, information architecture, content.
Period measured: 1–27 July 2026 against 1–27 August 2026, from Google Search Console.

The problem

The site had accumulated location pages the way most sites accumulate them: gradually, by different people, with no plan. Multiple URLs targeted the same city — /cork/, /it-support-cork/ and /it-support-cork-2/ all existed simultaneously. The copy across them was close to identical with the city name swapped, which is the doorway pattern Google filters rather than rewards. The pages competed with each other for the same searches, and none of them won.

What we did

  • Cut the location pages down to four. Cork, Galway, Limerick and Waterford — the four regions where Everything IT genuinely has something specific to offer. Everything else was retired and redirected rather than left to dilute the site.
  • Wrote each of the four differently. Not a template with the city swapped. Cork City and County Cork, Galway and the West, Limerick and the Mid-West, Waterford and the South-East each serve a different mix of local businesses, so each page targets the services that actually match its area.
  • Resolved the duplicate URLs. One canonical page per location, with 301s from the retired variants so the accumulated equity moved rather than evaporated.
  • Rebuilt internal linking between the location pages and the location directory, so the four surviving pages inherited the internal authority the retired ones had been splitting.

On individual commercial searches, the page-one movement is the part that matters more than the totals: business IT support Waterford averaged position 2.5, IT services Limerick around 5, IT support Limerick around 7, and network specialists Cork 9.5.

What did not go to plan

Two things, stated plainly because a case study that reports only the good half is an advertisement.

Limerick's average position got worse — 17.5 to 27.8 — while its impressions more than doubled. Those two facts are related. The page went from surfacing for a narrow set of searches to surfacing for a much wider set, and the newly-added ones sit lower down. An average dragged down by winning more ground is not the same as a decline, but it looks identical on a dashboard, and any agency reporting on this account should be able to tell you which one you are looking at.

Cork and Waterford both lost impressions even as their positions improved sharply. During a consolidation the retired URLs keep appearing in search results for a while, then stop; the totals dip before they recover. We expected this and said so in advance, which is the only reason it was not alarming when it happened.

Across the whole domain, including every retired URL bleeding its old traffic through a redirect, the same month looked flat. Judged only on the four pages that were actually the subject of the work, it did not. Both numbers are true, and an agency that shows you only the flattering one is choosing which truth to hand you.
