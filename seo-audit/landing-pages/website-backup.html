Why your host's backup isn't enough

Host-level backups usually exist for disaster recovery across the whole platform, not for restoring one customer's site to a specific hour. They can be slow to request, limited in retention, and stored on the same infrastructure as the site. If a plugin update breaks your checkout on a Friday afternoon, you want a restore point you control.

Backup, firewall and SSL do different jobs

Backups get you back online after something goes wrong. Website security stops most of those incidents happening in the first place, and SSL encrypts traffic in transit. Most business sites need all three. If you're on managed WordPress hosting, daily backups are already included.

FAQ

How often should a business site be backed up?

Daily for most sites. If you take orders or publish frequently, hourly is worth the difference, because a day of lost orders costs more than the plan does.

How fast is a restore?

Usually minutes. The slow part is deciding which restore point you want, which is why browsable history matters.

Does this work alongside a firewall?

Yes. Website security prevents most incidents; backup covers everything else.
